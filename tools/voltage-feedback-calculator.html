Formula and Method

This ideal negative voltage-feedback block model subtracts the feedback voltage at the summing junction: Verror = Vin − Vfb. The amplifier obeys Vout = Av × Verror, and the feedback network obeys Vfb = β × Vout.

  • Loop gain T = Av × β.
  • Closed-loop voltage gain Af = Av / (1 + Av × β).
  • Output voltage Vout = Af × Vin.
  • Feedback voltage Vfb = β × Vout.
  • Error voltage Verror = Vin − Vfb = Vin / (1 + Av × β).

Av and β are dimensionless voltage ratios (V/V). Input voltage is converted to volts before calculation: 1 mV = 0.001 V. The equivalent division form of error voltage avoids precision loss from subtracting nearly equal voltages. Internal numeric precision is retained; displayed values are rounded.

The denominator must be nonzero. A denominator too close to zero, overflow or underflow is rejected while the last valid result is preserved. Negative values follow the signed reference arrows. A negative signed loop gain is flagged as regenerative in this subtraction model; a finite algebraic result does not establish stability.

This is an ideal scalar voltage-feedback amplifier model, not a transistor-level or op-amp hardware circuit. No component values, output current, output power, bandwidth, saturation limits, loading or stability are assumed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is voltage feedback the same as current feedback?

No. Here Av relates output voltage to error voltage, and β relates feedback voltage to output voltage. The current-feedback tool instead models current ratios and currents. Neither tool simulates an actual amplifier device.

What happens when β is zero?

No feedback voltage is returned. Af equals Av, Vfb is zero, and Verror equals Vin.

Can the gain, feedback factor or input voltage be negative?

Yes. Values are signed relative to the diagram references, while the summing junction always subtracts Vfb. Regenerative signs are explicitly flagged; actual hardware stability cannot be established from these scalar inputs.

Why is a zero or nearly zero denominator rejected?

At 1 + Av × β = 0 the ideal feedback equation is singular. Near-zero denominators can produce numerically unreliable ideal gains; adjust Av or β to obtain a reliable finite solution.

What do the animated highlights represent?

They show the directed block-model signal paths, not electrons, physical propagation speed or a real-time frequency. Signed labels show voltage polarity. Motion follows the signal arrows for either polarity; zero signals stop.

Can this calculate output power or current?

No. Load, impedance, supply and device information are not provided. This tool calculates only the ideal voltage-feedback quantities shown.
