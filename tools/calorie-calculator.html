About this calculator

This combined calculator includes BMR, TDEE/maintenance energy, and user-selected calorie deficit or surplus modes. Estimates depend on the chosen equation and activity factor; they are not an individualized medical or nutrition prescription.

Calculation flow

Inputs → BMR equation → TDEE → Daily target

Formula and assumptions

Mifflin–St Jeor: men: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5; women: 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age − 161.

Revised Harris–Benedict: men: 88.362 + 13.397 × weight + 4.799 × height − 5.677 × age; women: 447.593 + 9.247 × weight + 3.098 × height − 4.330 × age.

TDEE estimate = BMR × selected activity factor. Maintenance target = TDEE; deficit target = TDEE − selected deficit; surplus target = TDEE + selected surplus.

Worked example

For a male aged 30, 70 kg, 175 cm, using Mifflin–St Jeor and a 1.55 activity factor, estimated BMR is about 1,649 kcal/day and TDEE is about 2,556 kcal/day. A selected 300 kcal deficit gives a target near 2,256 kcal/day. This is an arithmetic example, not a promise of weight change.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR estimates energy used at rest. TDEE estimates daily energy expenditure after multiplying BMR by an activity factor.

Can I change the method?

Yes. Choose Mifflin–St Jeor or Revised Harris–Benedict. Different equations can produce different estimates.

Does a calorie deficit guarantee weight loss?

No. Actual weight change depends on many factors and does not follow a precise fixed prediction from this calculator.

Is this medical advice?

No. The result is a general estimate. Seek qualified advice for individual dietary needs, medical conditions, pregnancy, adolescence or eating-disorder concerns.
