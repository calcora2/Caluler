Body Weight Change Calculator

Calculate absolute weight change, percentage change and average weekly change from two body-weight readings and elapsed days.

Track change between two weigh-ins

Starting weight, current weight and elapsed days determine absolute change, percentage change and average weekly rate.

Formula

Weight change = current weight − starting weight. Percentage change = weight change ÷ starting weight × 100. Average weekly change = weight change ÷ elapsed days × 7.

Start weight → Current weight → Change + percentage + weekly average

Worked example

A change from 80 kg to 76 kg over 28 days is −4 kg, or −5% of starting weight; the average change is −1 kg per week.

Limitations

Short-term weight fluctuates with fluid, food, glycogen and measurement conditions. The average is a mathematical summary of two readings, not a prediction or a recommended rate of weight change.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is weight change calculated?

Subtract starting weight from current weight. A positive result indicates gain; a negative result indicates loss.

What does average weekly change mean?

It divides the total change by elapsed days and multiplies by seven. It summarizes the two readings and is not a prediction of future change.

Why can daily weight fluctuate?

Fluid, food, glycogen, timing and measurement conditions can shift scale weight without representing a matching change in body fat.
