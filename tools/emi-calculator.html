About this Loan Amortization Calculator

Calculate loan repayments using durations measured in days, weeks, months or years. Leave the annual interest rate blank for a zero-interest loan, select daily, weekly, monthly or yearly payments, and optionally add extra principal payments. The calculator separates principal and interest for every payment period and estimates total repayment and interest savings.

How to use

  1. Enter the original loan amount.
  2. Enter the markup rate. Leave it blank for 0% interest. Select Annual rate (APR) for yearly rate input, or Per payment period when the rate applies each payment period (for example, 2% per month with monthly payments).
  3. Enter the loan term and select Days, Weeks, Months or Years.
  4. Select how often repayments occur: daily, weekly, monthly or yearly.
  5. Select one calculation method: Reducing balance — Standard EMI, Reducing balance — equal principal, or Flat markup — interest on original loan. The optional fixed-principal field is available in equal-principal and flat-markup modes; it can shorten the payoff period.
  6. Optionally enter additional principal to pay with each scheduled payment.
  7. Select Calculate Schedule. Review the totals and detailed repayment table.
  8. Use Copy Summary or Download CSV to save the results.

Calculation method

In both reducing-balance methods, interest is charged on the opening outstanding balance each payment period. Standard EMI estimates an approximately equal total installment; equal-principal mode splits principal evenly across the scheduled payments, so its total installment usually falls. Flat markup instead calculates each period's interest from the original loan amount, not the remaining balance. Without a fixed-principal entry, principal is divided evenly across the term; with a fixed-principal entry, the schedule can pay off early. Markup stays based on the original loan amount for each period charged; a shortened final period is prorated. With Annual rate (APR), the entered annual rate is prorated by period days over a 365.2425-day year. With Per payment period, the entered rate applies to one selected payment interval and is prorated if the final interval is shorter. A blank rate is treated as zero.

The calculator uses average durations of 7 days per week, 365.2425 days per year and 365.2425 divided by 12 days per month. If the selected loan term ends between scheduled payment dates, the final repayment period may be shorter than a full interval.

Worked example

For example, a 1,000-unit loan at 2% per monthly period over 12 months has three useful comparisons. Reducing balance with equal principal repays about 83.33 principal each month: markup starts at 20.00 and falls to about 1.67 in the last month, for about 130.00 total markup. Reducing-balance Standard EMI keeps the total installment about equal while interest declines with the balance. Flat markup calculates 20.00 markup each month on the original 1,000-unit amount; with no fixed principal entry, principal is about 83.33 per month, the installment is about 103.33, and total markup is 240.00 (total repayment 1,240.00). If you enter 100 units as principal per payment, reducing-balance equal-principal mode clears in 10 payments with markup falling from 20.00 to 2.00 (110.00 total markup); flat-markup mode clears in 10 payments with markup remaining 20.00 per charged month (200.00 total markup in this estimate). These are estimates, not lender quotes.

How extra payments work

An extra principal payment is added to each scheduled payment and applied to principal. This can shorten payoff. In flat-markup mode, each remaining period still uses the original loan amount as the markup base; if extra payments pay the loan off early, the calculator stops future period charges when the schedule ends. Actual lenders may apply different early-settlement rules.

Important assumptions

This calculator provides an estimate using a fixed rate and a proportional day-count method. Flat markup is calculated on the original loan amount for each scheduled payment period, while reducing-balance methods use the opening outstanding balance. Actual lenders may use different day-count conventions, compounding schedules, payment dates, fees, penalties, flat-interest rules, early-settlement terms or rounding. Verify the actual payoff amount with your lender before making financial decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I calculate a loan without interest?

Yes. Leave the markup/interest rate blank or enter 0. The calculator will calculate the repayment schedule without interest charges.

What is the difference between reducing-balance markup and flat markup?

Reducing-balance markup is calculated on the remaining principal, so the interest portion declines as the balance falls. Flat markup is calculated on the original loan amount for each payment period. For a 1,000-unit loan at 2% per month over 12 months, flat markup is 20 units per full month (240 units total when paid across 12 months), while reducing-balance equal principal starts at 20 units and ends near 1.67 units, with about 130 units total markup. You can enter 100 as principal per payment in either equal-principal or flat-markup mode; this estimate then charges each method's markup for the 10 periods needed to clear the loan. Actual lender conventions, flat-rate contracts and early-settlement rules can differ.

How is markup calculated on the outstanding balance each payment period?

For reducing-balance methods, period markup is calculated by multiplying the outstanding balance at the start of the payment period by the applicable period rate. When annual APR is selected, the annual rate is converted to the relevant period rate. Flat markup calculates each period's markup using the original loan amount instead.

What is the difference between Standard EMI and equal principal?

Standard EMI keeps the total installment approximately equal while its interest portion usually falls and its principal portion rises. Equal principal keeps principal repaid per period equal; because interest is calculated on the falling opening balance, the total installment usually declines over time.

Can I use days, weeks, months or years?

Yes. Select the loan term unit separately from the payment frequency. For example, a term can be entered in months with weekly repayments, or in days with daily repayments.

What does extra principal per payment mean?

It is the additional amount applied to the outstanding principal each time a regular payment is made. It can shorten the payoff period and lower interest in this estimate.

Why might the result differ from my lender?

Lenders may use specific calendar dates, daily compounding, fees, insurance, different rounding rules or other contract terms that this estimate does not include.

Are my loan details saved or sent anywhere?

The calculation is designed to run in your browser. Avoid entering private or sensitive information into any online tool unless you have reviewed the site's privacy information.
