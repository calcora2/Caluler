About the Profit Margin Calculator

Separate direct costs from operating expenses and other costs to understand gross profit, operating profit and net-of-entered-costs margin. The outputs depend on consistent accounting categories.

How to use

  1. Enter revenue for the same period as the costs.
  2. Enter direct costs, operating expenses and other costs separately.
  3. Review gross profit, operating profit, margin percentages and markup on direct cost.

Formula and method

Gross profit = revenue − direct costs. Operating profit = gross profit − operating expenses. Margin = profit ÷ revenue × 100. Markup = gross profit ÷ direct costs × 100.

Worked example

If revenue is 10,000 and direct costs are 4,500, gross profit is 5,500 and gross margin is 55%.

Assumptions and limitations

Use the same currency unit for all monetary inputs on this page. Results are estimates based on entered assumptions. They are not personalized financial, investment, credit, legal or tax advice; confirm important decisions with authoritative terms and a qualified professional where appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is margin?

Margin is profit as a percentage of revenue.

How is markup different?

Markup compares profit with cost, not sales revenue.

Does this include income tax?

Only costs entered here are included; tax treatment is not automatically inferred.

Why match the time periods?

Combining monthly revenue with annual costs distorts all profit and margin results.
