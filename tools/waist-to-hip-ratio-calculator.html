About the Waist to Hip Ratio Calculator

Waist-to-hip ratio divides waist circumference by hip circumference. Consistent measurement technique matters, and interpretation depends on the guidance and population being considered; the ratio alone is not a diagnosis.

How to use

  1. Measure waist and hip circumferences using the same unit and a consistent tape-measure technique.
  2. Enter both measurements in centimetres and calculate the ratio.
  3. Interpret the result as a screening measure, not a diagnosis. Measurement location and the reference guidance used can affect comparisons.

Formula and worked example

Waist-to-hip ratio = waist circumference ÷ hip circumference. With an 80 cm waist and 100 cm hips, the ratio is 0.80. The measurements must use the same unit.

Interpretation thresholds can vary by sex, age and guideline. This ratio is one screening measure and should not be used alone to diagnose a condition.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is waist-to-hip ratio calculated?

Divide waist circumference by hip circumference. Measure both using the same unit.

Where should I measure?

Keep the tape level around the waist and around the widest hip area without pulling it tightly.

Does the ratio diagnose a condition?

No. It is a screening indicator, not a diagnosis. This page accepts measurements from 20–250 cm.
