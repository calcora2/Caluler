12-minute Cooper test estimate

The Cooper test estimates aerobic capacity from distance completed in 12 minutes; a laboratory test measures oxygen uptake directly.

VO₂ Max Formula and Method

Cooper 12-minute run-test estimate: VO₂ max (ml/kg/min) = (distance in metres − 504.9) ÷ 44.73.

Worked example

For 2,400 metres, the estimate is (2,400 − 504.9) ÷ 44.73 ≈ 42.4 ml/kg/min.

How to use the result

Use the same test conditions when comparing results over time. Distance, pacing, course surface, weather and effort can change the estimate.

Limitations and safety

This is a field prediction, not a direct measurement of oxygen uptake in a laboratory. A 12-minute test can be strenuous; do not attempt maximal exertion if it is inappropriate for your health or fitness, and seek qualified advice when needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does VO₂ max mean?

VO₂ max describes the maximum rate of oxygen use during intense exercise, commonly expressed relative to body mass as millilitres per kilogram per minute.

How does the Cooper test estimate VO₂ max?

It uses the distance completed in 12 minutes in the equation (distance in metres − 504.9) ÷ 44.73.

Is this the same as a laboratory VO₂ max test?

No. The Cooper equation predicts a value from running performance; laboratory testing measures oxygen uptake directly under controlled conditions.
