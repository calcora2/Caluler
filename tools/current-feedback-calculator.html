Formula and Method

This ideal, scalar current-feedback model subtracts the feedback signal at the summing junction: Ierror = Iin − Ifb. The amplifier obeys Iout = Ai × Ierror, and the feedback network obeys Ifb = β × Iout.

  • Loop gain T = Ai × β.
  • Closed-loop current gain Acl = Ai / (1 + Ai × β).
  • Output current Iout = Acl × Iin.
  • Feedback current Ifb = β × Iout.
  • Error current Ierror = Iin − Ifb = Iin / (1 + Ai × β).

Ai and β are dimensionless current ratios (A/A). Input current is converted to amperes before calculation: 1 mA = 0.001 A. The equivalent division form of error current avoids loss of precision when nearly equal currents are subtracted. Full JavaScript numeric precision is retained internally; displayed values are rounded.

The denominator must be nonzero. Inputs that are too close to the singular denominator, overflow, or underflow are rejected. Negative currents follow the signed arrow references. Negative Ai × β gives regenerative signs in this subtraction model; a finite algebraic result does not establish stability. No frequency response, bandwidth, saturation, loading, supply limit or transistor behavior is modeled.

Feedback theory reference: Analog Devices: negative feedback and loop gain. This calculator applies the general block relationship to ideal current signals.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this a current-feedback op-amp simulator?

No. It is an ideal scalar current-gain feedback block model. A current-feedback op-amp requires additional device, impedance and frequency information.

What happens when β is zero?

No feedback is returned. Closed-loop gain equals Ai, feedback current is zero, and error current equals the input current.

Can Ai, β or the input current be negative?

Yes, they are signed quantities relative to the diagram references. The summing junction still subtracts Ifb. A negative signed loop gain is flagged as regenerative; no stability claim is made.

Why is a denominator near zero rejected?

The ideal equations become singular at 1 + Ai × β = 0. Very small denominators are numerically unreliable and can produce unbounded ideal gains; the calculator preserves the last valid result and shows validation.

Do the moving highlights show physical electron speed?

No. They indicate the signed schematic signal paths. Zero signals stop and negative signals reverse relative to the reference arrows. No physical drift velocity or frequency is inferred.
