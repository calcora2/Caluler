About the Heart Rate Zone Calculator

The Karvonen method estimates training zones from an age-predicted maximum heart rate and resting pulse. These ranges are exercise guides, not medical thresholds; medicines, health conditions and individual variation can affect heart rate.

How to use

  1. Enter your age and resting heart rate in beats per minute (bpm), measured while rested.
  2. Calculate the zones and check the displayed bpm ranges. This page estimates maximum heart rate using 220 minus age before applying heart-rate reserve.
  3. Use the values as general training guides rather than medical targets. If a health condition or medication affects heart rate, ask a qualified clinician about appropriate exercise intensity.

Formula and worked example

The current method estimates maximum heart rate as 220 − age, then uses heart-rate reserve: estimated maximum heart rate − resting heart rate. The zones are calculated as resting heart rate plus 50–60%, 60–70%, 70–80%, 80–90% and 90–100% of that reserve.

For age 30 and resting heart rate 60 bpm, estimated maximum heart rate is 190 bpm and reserve is 130 bpm. This is a simplified estimate; exercise targets should be individualized, especially with cardiac conditions or heart-rate-affecting medication.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are the five zones estimated?

Maximum heart rate is estimated as 220 minus age. Each zone then uses a percentage of heart-rate reserve above resting heart rate.

Are these universal training limits?

No. Age-based equations are estimates; fitness, medication and health conditions can affect appropriate exercise intensity.

Which inputs are accepted?

Enter age from 18–100 years and resting heart rate from 30–120 bpm. Resting rate must be below estimated maximum heart rate.
